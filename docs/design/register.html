01 · Concept

A register, not a dashboard.

PolicyPilot keeps a bound record of rules, decisions and approvals, and every entry in it says where it came from. The interface is drawn like that record: a sheet carries the work, a paper margin carries what annotates it, ink does the talking, colour is spent only on decisions, and three marks say who did what: the model's dotted circle proposes, the engine's solid square decides, and a person's seal approves.

P1

Sheet and margin

One working surface with hairline structure; whatever annotates it (a paragraph, a finding, a trace, a rule's provenance) sits in the paper margin beside it. No card inside a card; no shadow under anything that does not float; no sentence under a title explaining the title.

P2

Colour is a decision

Green, red and amber appear only beside the words Approved, Declined and Manual review, and on a changed cell in a diff. Chrome is ink on paper. The brand blue means one thing: this is selected or open. It never fills a button.

P3

Who did what

Three actors, three marks, and a plain outlined square for the system itself. Proposals and drafts are dashed; what the engine may run is solid; what a person approved carries a seal. A model's proposal can never be mistaken for a published rule, and a model is never named as an author.

P4

Provenance in one line

Every screen, drawer and trace carries one monospace line: rule set · version · status · time · by whom. It never wraps. The audit log is that line kept forever, and it is append-only.

P5

Numbers behave

Tabular lining figures, a true minus (U+2212), 150,000 ₪ in Hebrew and ₪150,000 in English chrome. A number that fed a decision is never rounded, truncated or cut by an ellipsis.

P6

Two scripts, one law

English chrome, Hebrew content, one type family for both. A Hebrew value never shares a row with an English label; marks sit on the reading-start side; every machine token inside Hebrew is isolated and never wraps.

The brief names three people, and the interview adds two. The chrome is English by the brief's decision (Israeli hi-tech, ESI's own material); every sentence that carries meaning for the first three is Hebrew: labels, reasons, findings, answers, the policy itself. 03 Typography and the bidi law

IBM Plex is one design programme in which Sans, Sans Hebrew, Mono and Serif share identical vertical metrics and a 600-unit digit width, so a column of amounts in the sans and a column of rule ids in the mono align to the same grid. The Hebrew is by Yanek Iontef; its letters stand at 0.89 of the Latin cap height, which is why R-330 and 150,000 ₪ sit inside a Hebrew sentence without shouting. Frank Ruhl Libre (also Iontef) sets policy paragraphs: the source document reads as a document, and everything derived from it reads as an instrument. Inter and Heebo are retired. In the product the five families are self-hosted from npm (@fontsource/ibm-plex-sans, @fontsource/ibm-plex-sans-hebrew, @fontsource/ibm-plex-mono, @fontsource/ibm-plex-serif, @fontsource/frank-ruhl-libre), only the weights used (400, 500, 600; mono 400, 500; serif 400, 500), with font-display: swap; this file links Google Fonts only so that it opens anywhere.

The bidi law

  1. The document is lang="en" dir="ltr". Every Hebrew block (paragraph, answer, rule label, finding, reason, explanation) gets lang="he" dir="rtl" in markup, never by CSS alone.
  2. A Hebrew value never shares a row with an English label. Label above, value below (.kv); or the whole block is Hebrew; or the value hangs from the row's start edge in a box that shrinks to its text (.he-quote). No zigzag.
  3. Marks sit on the reading-start side. In a Hebrew thread the person's and the model's marks are on the right, and the thread is a right-to-left container inside the left-to-right chrome.
  4. dir="auto" only where the direction is unknown: the question composer, the change request, the acknowledgement note, and a rule-set switcher whose options are Hebrew. Never on a block whose language is known.
  5. Every machine token inside Hebrew is isolated and never wraps: rule ids, field names, amounts with sign and symbol, dates, versions, ranges, expressions. A bare <bdi> is dir="auto", and a token without letters (8,000 ₪, −12, 9%) has no strong character, so Chromium puts its symbol on the wrong side; numeric tokens always carry dir="ltr". Parentheses belong inside the isolate, not around it.
  6. A citation chip sits after the sentence's punctuation, at most one per claim inline; the rest go to the sources strip. Chips keep 4px of air on each side inside Hebrew.
  7. Logical properties only: text-align: start, margin-inline-start, inset-inline-end, border-inline-start. The one physical property is the .he-quote bar, which must stay on the row's edge.
  8. Tables keep LTR column order. Hebrew text cells are dir="rtl" with text-align: start; numeric cells are LTR and end-aligned; id cells are mono LTR.
  9. Mirror only what encodes reading direction (chevrons, next and back); never clocks, checks, play, or the seal.
  10. Generated Hebrew uses the right characters: gershayim ש״ח (U+05F4), en dash for ranges (18–65), U+2212 for negatives, NBSP between ¶ and its number. Bidi control characters (U+202A–U+202E, U+2066–U+2069) are stripped from anything the model or a user supplies that becomes a label or an identifier.

05 Controls

One primary action per screen, drawn in ink on paper (paper on ink in the dark theme). Everything else is secondary, quiet or a link. A disabled button is never the end of the story: the reason is text beside it, or the button stays enabled and opens the box that explains what is missing. Labels are verbs; an object follows when the verb alone is ambiguous ("Ask", "Close", "Retry" stand alone; "Publish version 2", "Run 200 cases" do not).

Buttons

Publishing waits: 7 findings must be acknowledged.
  • Disabled primary is an outline with ink-3 text, so in the dark theme it never reads as a third grey beside the secondary; the reason stands beside it in 12px.
  • Busy replaces the label with a spinner and keeps the width; the button stays where it was.
  • Heights: 32px; 36px for the single primary action in a workspace header; 28px inside toolbars, table rows and findings. Icons only when they add meaning (add, open externally); no icon on Run, Publish or Ask.

06 Chips, tags and marks

Three silhouettes: a mono rectangle is any identifier (a rule, a case, a change, a finding, a version, a sandbox); a serif pill is a paragraph of the policy; an outlined glyph is a tool call. Decision tags are the only coloured words. Everything is 20px tall, so it sits inside a Hebrew sentence and a table row alike. A chip that opens something is an anchor: focusable, hover shows the cited text, click opens the target in the margin and flashes it once.

07 Tables

Tables are the product. A paper header row with one rule under it, 36px rows separated by hairlines, no vertical dividers, no zebra stripes, no outer border, nothing centred. The header is sticky, the first and last columns can freeze, the active sort is the only loud header, the selected row carries a 2px bar in the brand blue at its start edge, and an empty cell is a visible dash. A list of 200 rows lives in a sheet that scrolls inside the screen, with its count in the footer; past a thousand rows the body is virtualised.

12 Do and don't: the review checklist

Every pull request that touches the interface is reviewed against this list. The "don't" column is the fingerprint of an interface nobody designed; each item there is a hard rejection, not a matter of taste.

Do

  • Draw every colour, size and duration from a token; add a token before adding a value; no inline styles in components.
  • Put the work on one sheet and its annotations in the paper margin; separate with hairlines and tone.
  • Use green, red and amber only beside Approved, Declined, Manual review and on a changed cell in a diff; always with a glyph and a word.
  • Mark who did what: dotted circle, solid square, silhouette, outlined square; dashed for proposals and drafts, the seal for a person's decision; never name a model as an author.
  • Give every screen, drawer and trace a provenance line that never wraps; give every row a way to reach the paragraph or rule it came from, and every chip a target that flashes once.
  • Set numbers in tabular lining figures, end-aligned under an end-aligned header, one precision per column, U+2212 minus, ₪ after the number in Hebrew; never cut a number.
  • Follow the bidi law: lang="he" dir="rtl" on every Hebrew block, label above value, marks on the reading-start side, isolated tokens that never wrap.
  • Use the glossary's words and the product's own sentences ("Publishing waits: …", "Nothing was stored.").
  • State beside a disabled button why it is disabled; design loading, empty, refused and stale states before the happy path.
  • Keep the type floor: 12px chrome, 13px Hebrew, 16px serif; keep contrast: 4.5:1 text, 3:1 marks, in both themes, tested.
  • One primary action per screen, in ink; motion only at 120/180ms, the seal, and the one flash.
  • Light by default (a projector, an unknown machine, a bank's meeting room); dark is a toggle remembered per browser, never the first impression.
  • Every persona's five-second question (section 01) is answered on its screen without a click: the sentence for the applicant, the version that decided, the paragraph behind a rule.

Don't

  • No Inter, Roboto, Heebo, Arial, system-ui; no purple, indigo or violet; no gradients anywhere; no gradient text.
  • No explanatory subline under a title; no motto in the chrome; no counts in the navigation that are not something to act on.
  • No cards inside cards, no rounded-xl, no drop shadows on panels, no glassmorphism, no blurred blobs, no dotted backgrounds, no tinted alert boxes with an icon on the left.
  • No dark sidebar; no icon-with-label nav; no icon in a tinted rounded square; no emoji; no sparkles glyph; no switches.
  • No row of four KPI cards; no pie or donut; no chart with two axes; no zero figure taking a quarter of a row.
  • No tracked-out uppercase labels or status tags (the seal alone keeps capitals); no "→" welded to links; no exclamation marks; no "Get started", "Submit", "Learn more"; no "cache" or "tokens" in a credit officer's chrome.
  • No chat bubbles, no avatars, no typing animation; no spinner in the middle of a screen; no shimmer; no dashed empty-state frame.
  • No centred numbers, no proportional figures in a column, no colour as the only carrier of a status, no red pill on something that is not a decision.
  • No zebra stripes, no vertical dividers, no outer table border, no hidden columns without a scrollbar and a count.
  • No English label sharing a row with a Hebrew value; no ellipsis through a number; no bare <bdi> around an amount.
  • No fade-in on every section, no hover scale, no bounce; no modal for a detail view; no toast for something the screen should say in place; no lorem ipsum or placeholder people.
